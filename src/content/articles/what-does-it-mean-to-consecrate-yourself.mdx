We often pray for God to move in our lives, our families, and our churches. We want to see people saved, lives transformed, and God do things that only He can do.

But are we willing to allow Him to work in us?

That question sits at the heart of consecration. **To consecrate yourself to God means to set your life apart for Him—to surrender your thoughts, decisions, relationships, desires, and priorities to His authority.** It is not a way to earn His love. It is a response to the grace and mercy He has already shown us through Jesus.

Joshua 3:5 gives us a picture of that response and challenges us to consider a personal question: Am I fully surrendered to Jesus today?

## What Joshua 3:5 meant at the Jordan River

After forty years in the wilderness, Israel stood at the edge of the Jordan River, preparing to enter the land God had promised them. But the river was overflowing its banks. They faced an obstacle they could not overcome in their own strength.

Before they crossed, Joshua gave this instruction:

> “Then Joshua said to the people, ‘Consecrate yourselves, for tomorrow the LORD will do wonders among you.’” — Joshua 3:5 ESV

This instruction belonged to a particular moment in Israel’s history. It was not a formula promising that every act of surrender will produce a miracle the next day. God had already promised Israel the land. Their consecration did not earn that promise or force His hand; it was an obedient response to the God who had spoken.

There is a principle here for our walk with Christ: God calls His people to holiness and surrender. We do not know what tomorrow holds, but we do know He calls us to be faithful today.

## 1. Consecrate yourself through daily surrender

Consecration is not just a one-time spiritual experience. It is a daily posture before God.

In Romans 12:1, Paul appeals to believers on the basis of God’s mercy, calling them to offer their bodies as living sacrifices. Every part of life belongs to Him—not only the parts that look religious.

It is possible to attend church, serve in ministry, and stay busy with spiritual activities while still holding areas of our lives back from God. We may offer Him our Sunday mornings while keeping control of our decisions, our relationships, or our plans.

Jesus calls His followers to deny themselves, take up their cross daily, and follow Him (Luke 9:23). Notice that word: daily. Following Jesus means continually choosing obedience over our own desires.

Daily consecration can look like:

- Making time for prayer and Scripture.
- Choosing obedience when it conflicts with what we want.
- Turning away from sin rather than making excuses for it.
- Surrendering our plans and priorities to God.

These practices are not a checklist for earning God’s approval. They are ways of responding to His mercy. Consecration should shape how we live Monday through Saturday, not just what we do on Sunday.

## 2. Go deeper by obeying God today

Sometimes we become so focused on what we want God to do next that we overlook what He is asking of us right now.

We want to grow spiritually, but are we spending time in His Word? We want stronger families, but are we leading our families in the things of God? We want revival, but are we willing to repent and draw closer to Him?

James 4:8 calls us to draw near to God. Growing deeper in our relationship with Christ requires intentionality: prayer, the study of Scripture, and a willingness to respond in obedience.

Later in Joshua 3, the priests carrying the ark step into the Jordan, God stops the waters, and the people cross on dry ground (Joshua 3:13–17). The priests could not make the river stop flowing. Only God could do that. Their responsibility was to trust and obey.

**We cannot manufacture a move of God. But we can live faithfully before Him.**

What is God calling you to do today? Perhaps you have been putting off a difficult conversation, reconciliation with someone, or an area of your spiritual life that needs attention. Do not become so focused on tomorrow that you neglect obedience today.

If you are struggling to release your plans, consider this related reflection on [trusting God to direct your steps](/articles/trust-god-to-direct-your-steps/).

## 3. Invite God to search your heart

Consecration requires honesty. In Psalm 139:23–24, David invites God to search his heart, examine his thoughts, and lead him in the way everlasting.

That is not always a comfortable prayer. We can grow accustomed to attitudes, habits, and compromises that Scripture calls us to turn away from. Over time, we may begin defending the very things we need to surrender.

Ask yourself:

- Is pride shaping my choices?
- Am I holding on to unforgiveness?
- Have I allowed sin or compromise to become normal?
- Am I trying to control something God is calling me to surrender?
- Have I become spiritually complacent?

Consecration does not mean pretending to be perfect. It means being honest before God, confessing sin, and submitting ourselves to Him. First John 1:9 reminds us of His faithfulness to forgive and cleanse those who confess their sins.

Our pursuit of holiness is not simply preparation for something God might do in the future. It concerns our relationship with Him right now. First Peter 1:15–16 calls us to holiness because the One who called us is holy.

**God is worthy of our surrender regardless of what tomorrow brings.**

## A life set apart for Christ

At the Jordan, Israel faced an obstacle beyond its own strength. God remained faithful to His promise, and His people were called to respond in obedience.

As followers of Jesus, we are called to live set apart for Him every day—not only during significant spiritual moments. We can spend so much time asking what God will do tomorrow that we neglect what He is calling us to do today.

My prayer is that we would become people who pursue the Lord wholeheartedly, hunger for His Word, walk in obedience, and live surrendered to Him. Not because we are trying to earn something from God, but because He is worthy of our lives.

Before moving on, take a quiet moment with these three questions:

1. Am I living a life of daily surrender to God?
2. What is God calling me to obey today?
3. What area of my life have I not fully surrendered to Christ?

Ask the Lord to search your heart and reveal anything you have been holding back. Whatever He brings to your attention, respond in repentance, faith, and obedience. You do not have to wait for a more convenient time to follow Him.
